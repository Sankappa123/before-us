# BEFORE US — Master Vision

*Two lives. One world. The rise of civilization.*

Status: planning draft, 2026-11-11.

## One paragraph

Two people walk into an untouched prehistoric valley with nothing. They learn the land, make their first tools, light their first fire, and build their first shelter. Over time, what they build and what they learn outlasts them and grows into a settlement, a people and a civilization. It is a third-person, photorealistic, cooperative game for exactly two players to begin with, built in Unreal Engine 5.

## Pillars

1. **A world worth standing still in.** Geography that obeys nature, light and weather that change the mood, sound that is alive. Quality over size.
2. **Believable people.** Two specific, expressive humans who move with weight and look right in every light.
3. **Two together.** The game is designed around a pair: carrying, hunting, building and deciding together. Never a solo game with a guest.
4. **Earned progress.** Every step forward comes from understanding the world: observing, experimenting, making by hand.
5. **A living ecosystem that remembers.** Animals have needs and habits; the land responds to what the players take from it.
6. **Peace is a complete path.** Conflict is possible later, never required.

## How it should feel

- **First hour:** small, quiet and slightly afraid. Cold hands, a loud river, something moving in the trees.
- **First night by the fire:** relief and pride.
- **Much later:** looking down from the pass at smoke rising from a village that started as that one fire.

## What it is not

- Not an MMO. Two players, one host.
- Not a combat game with crafting attached.
- Not a checklist of survival meters.
- Not a huge empty map.
- Not a copy of any existing game's assets, code, characters or world.

## The honest scale of the ambition

The full vision (camp → village → kingdom → advanced civilization, at the visual quality of the reference games) is the output of studios with hundreds of people over many years. Two people cannot build all of it at that quality.

What two people **can** build, with free world-class tools (Unreal, MetaHuman, Megascans, Epic's animation sample) and patience, is a small, exceptional slice of it: one valley, two characters, one day's survival loop, working in co-op. That slice is the product for the first year. Everything after it is decided by what the slice teaches.

This is not a reason to lower the visual ambition. It is the reason to keep the area and feature count small so the quality can be high.

## Long-term arc (direction, not commitment)

| Era | Core experience |
|---|---|
| Arrival | Survive a day and a night |
| Camp | Tools, storage, seasons, hunting |
| Home | Permanent shelter, crafts, tamed animals |
| People | Others join; knowledge is recorded and passed on |
| Settlement | Farming, specialised work, trade |
| Beyond | Villages, alliances, optional conflict, later ages |

The step from "two people" to "a people" needs a design answer (other humans joining, generational time skips, or both). See OPEN_QUESTIONS.md.

## Reference works

Studied for technique and feel, never for assets or code: Red Dead Redemption 2 (world immersion, wildlife), Ghost of Tsushima (wind, grass, composition), Horizon (procedural vegetation), The Last of Us Part II (animation), Kingdom Come: Deliverance II (grounded nature), Assassin's Creed Origins/Odyssey/Valhalla (traversal, landscapes), Sons of the Forest (physical building), Ancestors: The Humankind Odyssey (discovery), The Long Dark, Subnautica, Valheim, Green Hell, theHunter: Call of the Wild (animal behaviour).
